What it shows

Every byte, every app, every address.

NetPull reads Windows' own network events, so it knows exactly which app sent or received each byte. No driver, no firewall changes.

Live graph

Download and upload, second by second, with 3 hours, a day, a week or a month of history. Click any point to see which apps were busy.

Usage by app

Today, this week, this month or 90 days. Click an app to see the servers it talked to, or flip to hosts to see it the other way round.

Connections

Every open connection with its host name, country, ISP, port and live speed. Search it, sort it, look an address up.

Devices on your network

Quiet checks every 5 minutes find what's on your Wi-Fi and wired network. Name them, trust them, and hear about newcomers.

Pick an adapter

See everything, or just Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Tailscale or your VPN, so nothing gets counted twice.

Mini graph and tray

A floating speed graph you can drag anywhere, and a tray icon that is itself a tiny live graph.

Privacy

It watches your network. Nobody watches it.

Stays on your PC

History lives in a small database in your own app data folder. There's no account and nothing is uploaded.

Lookups you control

Countries and ISPs come from ip-api.com and device makers from macvendors.com. Only remote addresses are sent, and both can be switched off.

Watch only

NetPull never blocks, changes your firewall or installs a driver. It asks for admin rights once, at install, to read Windows' network events.

Questions

Good to know

Why does it need admin rights?

Windows only lets administrators read the network events that say which app owns each byte. The installer sets up a startup task, so you're asked once and never again.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC".

The installer isn't code-signed. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Can it set a data cap?

Yes. Set your monthly allowance and the day your bill resets, and NetPull warns you at a level you pick and again when you hit it. It's off until you turn it on.

Does it slow my PC down?

No. It adds traffic up once a second in memory and writes to its history once a minute.

How long is history kept?

90 days by default, or 30 days or a year if you prefer. Second-by-second detail is for the live graph; minute detail is kept for a week.
